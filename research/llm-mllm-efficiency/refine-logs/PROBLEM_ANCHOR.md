# Problem Anchor (frozen 2026-10-17 — copied verbatim into every round)

- **Bottom-line problem**: Efficient-reasoning work reports *token reduction* as its efficiency gain, and decode accelerators (speculative decoding, KV-cache eviction) report their own gains on *uncompressed* reasoners. In deployment they are stacked. We do not know whether these gains compose, because all three may draw on the same resource — the redundancy (restatement) inside long chains of thought.
- **Must-solve bottleneck**: There is no controlled measurement of how length-penalty RL changes (i) the draftability of the remaining trace and (ii) the robustness of signal-free KV eviction. The leading explanation for why random eviction works on reasoning (Random Attention 2609.03430: "the model restates what it still needs") is, in its authors' words, *inferred, not measured*.
- **Non-goals**: proposing a new length-compression RL method, a new drafter architecture, or a new eviction policy; long-input (long-prompt) workloads; MLLMs; training any model larger than a draft model.
- **Constraints**: ≤ 8 × A100-80GB / RTX 4090-class GPUs; open checkpoints only; main experiments ≤ ~2 GPU-weeks; inference-dominant; target venue NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR main (realistic: main-track borderline or *ACL Findings / MLSys).
- **Success condition**: On ≥ 3 verbose→compressed parent/child pairs at 2 scales, we can state with CIs (a) how much of the token saving survives as wall-clock saving once SD is on, decomposed into intrinsic draftability loss vs drafter mismatch vs attention-cost savings; and (b) whether signal-free eviction stays free on compressed traces — with a *directly measured* restatement statistic that predicts both (a) and (b) — or a clean refutation of that shared-substrate story.
